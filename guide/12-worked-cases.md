## Case A: motion with a light, open refrain

**Brief.** Moderately dense verses move from hesitation to confidence. A short repeated phrase should open outward; the lead must stay clear.

**Chosen relationships.** Bass supports kick arrivals and replies in their gaps. A two-note hook waits for the words; refrain chord length and width supply the lift.

**STYLE OF MUSIC**

```text
Brisk straight pulse with a firm kick and snare, fine high percussion, and short tuneful bass notes that meet the kick then answer its gaps. Verses use clipped low-mid chords, leaving space around the lead; a bright two-note synth figure replies after each line. One clear midrange lead sings a recurring stepwise verse tune with crisp consonants, then opens into longer vowels at the refrain. There the chords lengthen and spread wide while the bass sustains more fully. Add restrained upper harmonies only on the final return. Keep the lead close and dry, drums punchy, and the wider backing softly reverberant; end with the two-note figure and a short fading tail.
```

**EXCLUDE:** none

**SONG TITLE:** Room to Move

**Prediction.** The answer figure should occupy word gaps and the refrain should widen around a consistently near lead.

## Case B: a private admission in an empty room

**Brief.** Short, sparse verses move from admission to a calm promise. The brief asks for a quiet near voice and a distant instrumental answer, without a drum-led pulse.

**Chosen relationships.** A compact verse tune anchors the words. Piano carries harmonic time; a remote wind tone answers rather than competes. The final return grows through vowel length, not louder percussion.

**STYLE OF MUSIC**

```text
One close lower-midrange lead sings the verses on a recurring three-note contour, holding the last stressed vowel of each short line. Widely spaced piano chords move slowly and leave the pauses exposed. A distant muted trumpet gives a single soft answer after the second line of each verse; keep its long decay behind the voice. At the repeated promise, the vocal contour rises one step and the vowels lengthen while the piano adds an upper note. On the final return add one quiet harmony only at line endings. Keep the vocal nearly dry and the piano room large but quiet; finish with the sung lead alone, followed by the last piano resonance.
```

**EXCLUDE:** drum-led beat

**SONG TITLE:** The Room Keeps Quiet

**Prediction.** The vocal should remain distinctly pitched and near, while the instrumental replies suggest greater distance. The last return should feel steadier through phrasing.

**Chosen relationships.** Defined drum and bass attacks carry motion. A small verse pitch pattern accommodates dense syllables; guitar only occupies its gaps, then reinforces the broader refrain tune.

**STYLE OF MUSIC**

```text
Fast-feeling straight pulse with hard kick and snare arrivals, short closed high percussion, and clipped bass notes locked to the downbeats. A narrow recurring verse melody carries the dense words on definite sung pitches, with sharp consonants and brief held phrase endings. Low rough-edged guitar chords strike between the lines over a small chord cycle. The simpler refrain opens into wider melodic intervals and sustained vowels; a higher guitar doubles its final two notes, while the rhythm guitars spread and the bass notes lengthen. Keep the lead forward and centered, drum attacks distinct, and guitar roughness behind the words. End together on the final sung word with a short chord decay.
```

## Case D: a cut that makes the return count

**Brief.** The lyric has two compact verses, a repeated release phrase, and an explicitly labeled break. The brief asks for an electronic arrival after a very short all-sound gap.

**Chosen relationships.** Keep the pulse rate stable while felt weight changes. Low end yields at the kick attack; the cut stops both sources and tails. Restore the same hook with a larger carrier.

**STYLE OF MUSIC**

```text
A compact sung verse motif sits over broad backbeat accents, clipped chord stabs, and a low synth note that dips briefly at each kick attack. Keep the voice clean and centered, with phrase endings held on definite pitches. During the labeled break, let high percussion become finer while a filtered version of the hook rises. Cut every source and effect tail for half a beat immediately before the release phrase. Return on a regular four-beat kick pattern with the same hook an octave higher and doubled by a bright sustained synth; the singer opens the refrain into longer notes. Keep bass weight compact, upper chords wide, and rough digital fills confined to the gaps between vocal lines.
```

**CHARACTER COUNT:** 692

**EXCLUDE:** none

**SONG TITLE:** Half a Beat of Air

**Prediction.** The unchanged hook should make the return recognizable, while the short total cut and changed accent weight should mark its arrival. The silence is an intended event, not a verified render.

**Chosen relationships.** Assign different roles rather than duplicate a source inventory: plucks mark offbeats, electronics sustain harmony, and bass fills open spaces. Refrain width changes without replacing the acoustic identity.

**STYLE OF MUSIC**

```text
Warm plucked acoustic guitar marks the offbeats while a soft electronic chord bed sustains beneath it. Use an even moderate pulse, short low drum attacks, and rounded bass replies only in the guitar rests. A clear lead sings a small rising-and-falling verse tune with natural word stress and slightly lengthened line endings. The refrain carries the same contour higher, holding its opening vowel as the electronic chords widen; the guitar keeps its dry rhythmic role. Let a bright filtered synth answer the refrain in the vocal gap, then disappear for the next verse. Keep acoustic pick detail near, sustained electronics farther back, and the voice distinct at the center. Finish on guitar and one sung phrase.
```

**CHARACTER COUNT:** 712

**EXCLUDE:** none

**SONG TITLE:** Thread by Thread

## Case F: one hook passed between sources

**Brief.** The lyric has an opening memory, two sung verses, and a recurring line of recognition. The brief asks for a memorable small hook that changes carrier as recognition develops.

**Chosen relationships.** Hold the motif constant while its carrier changes. The first source introduces it, a second replies to the voice, and both briefly double the later refrain; keep continuous counterlines out of the verse.

**STYLE OF MUSIC**

```text
Begin with a small piano motif, then leave it incomplete when the lead enters. The verses are softly sung on a compact descending contour, with held vowels at each line ending over lightly brushed percussion and a slowly moving bass. A bowed string completes the piano motif only after the second line, keeping the voice in the foreground. At the recognition refrain, the lead turns the contour upward and the piano quietly doubles its hook; on the later return the string joins that same melody an octave above. Keep verse harmony spare, broaden the sustained chords only at the refrain, and give the string a longer room tail than the dry piano attacks. Close by returning the motif to piano alone.
```

**CHARACTER COUNT:** 700

**EXCLUDE:** none

**SONG TITLE:** Recognize the Light

**Prediction.** The hook should remain identifiable through piano, string, and vocal roles. The later doubling should communicate recognition without adding a competing tune.

**Chosen relationships.** Keep low accents dependable and let short answers create movement around them. Change percussion articulation at the refrain instead of speeding the song; scope projected singing and extra width there.

**STYLE OF MUSIC**

```text

Dry guitar chops sit with the snare, with light unequal high subdivisions and brushed percussion in the verses. One low-mid lead sings a recurring four-note verse tune, placing each syllable on a clear pitch and gently sustaining the phrase ends. A bright sliding string answers only in the pauses. When the promise returns, switch the brushes to firmer stick attacks, widen the soft acoustic chord bed, and let the lead project a longer rising refrain melody. Keep voice and bass close, the answers slightly farther away, and the short vocal room unobtrusive. End with the string's brief answer after the last sung line.
```

## Case H: a persistent tone and a quiet withdrawal

**Brief.** A lyric moves from urgency toward acceptance, with a short recurring phrase and a final verse already present in the finished text. The brief wants tension to diminish without inventing a triumphant final chorus.

**Chosen relationships.** Preserve one source identity while changing its envelope and register. Reduce rhythmic weight and width for the final verse; retain sung pitch after the backing thins.

**STYLE OF MUSIC**

```text
A dark sustained synth tone sits behind dry drum strikes and a short repeating bass figure. The lead sings the verses on a restrained ascending melody, with clipped syllabic notes settling into a held vowel at each phrase end. At the recurring acceptance line, the same synth rises in register, softens its attack, and spreads into a quiet chord; the vocal repeats its melody with longer notes. Give the earlier instrumental gap one brief saturated burst, then restore the clean lead foreground. For the final verse, withdraw bass and drums, narrow the synth, and shorten its decay while the voice keeps its pitched contour. Let the last sung note finish before the remaining tone dies into silence.
```

**CHARACTER COUNT:** 699

**EXCLUDE:** none

**SONG TITLE:** What Remains Near

**Prediction.** The recurring source should change character without losing identity. The final verse should remain sung while the accompaniment releases its weight.

## How to select a case

Use the relationships that fit the current lyric, not the nearest bundle of adjectives. Cases A and G both emphasize groove but give bass and percussion different jobs. Cases B and H both withdraw backing but differ in depth and sustained-source development. Cases C and F expand a hook by doubling it, while D changes its register after a cut. Case E keeps two source families present together instead of inserting a separate contrast section. Each sung case defines verse pitch before relying on diction or mix distance.

The exact sources, pulse, cuts, and section order remain choices. A poem with no refrain does not acquire one because these examples contain refrains. EXCLUDE stays separate and can be `none`; the positive Style establishes the song.
