# 11 — Written inheritance, relevance and contract audit

This audit concerns the intended design and its final text. The writer has no audio access and cannot certify what a generated song sounds like. No listening task, scorecard or recording request is a prerequisite.

## Inheritance and relevance

Privately ask which choices follow current instructions; which follow confirmed taste; which are original proposals justified by this song; and which arrived only because a case, profile or catalog label made them salient. Keep evidence status separate from musical usefulness. A reference-derived relation can be excellent without proving an isolated preference; an original idea needs no reference ancestry to be eligible.

Does every significant source and section instruction have a job? Would the design remain coherent if teaching cases were unavailable? Did catalog consultation reveal useful organization or merely add a label? Did forced novelty displace a better fitting familiar approach? Keep fitting similarities; revise unexplained inheritance. No difference quota or duty to use research.

## Musical coherence from text

| Inspect | Written question |
| --- | --- |
| Structure | Does the named stanza, refrain or bridge exist? Is an instrumental proposal distinct from text repetition? |
| Time | Is pulse, shared flexible time or pulse-free phrasing intentional? Are meter, subdivision and phrase length distinct? |
| Harmony | Are chord-change rate, voicing, bass destination, cadence and tonal movement coherently related where material? |
| Melody | Is verse singing positively pitched and recurring? Does any transformation retain the intended cell or contour without copying a source tune? |
| Ensemble | Are one foreground, inner tune, coequal lines or rotating ownership actually specified when important? Is overlap purposeful rather than automatically prohibited? |
| Voice | Do register, force, articulation, duration and support have appropriate independent scopes? Are special modes explicitly requested/tagged? |
| Development | Is growth, stasis, transformation, subtraction or contrast chosen for this form instead of inherited as a duty? |
| Image | Are width, depth, texture and persistence attached to roles? Can requested tails coexist with cuts and chord changes? |
| End | Does the ending follow the supplied text without obligatory new refrain, crescendo, fade or silence? |

Do not demand an answer to every row in the final paragraph. The audit detects material conflicts, not missing checklist clauses. A brief with no tempo, chord map or exact instrument roster can still be complete.

## Recent-output specificity check

Where recent prompts are available, check for an unexplained repeated musical bundle, not merely repeated words or genre labels. Preserve fitting similarities; revise unsupported carryover. Confirm concrete part behavior where it defines this song, an explicitly recurring pitched verse, and no narrative-claim explanation replacing sound design. Do not claim to know absent history. A different instrument name over the same organization is not by itself a meaningful repair. For an open brief, check that catalog discovery or available craft knowledge informed a concrete direction, without requiring a genre rotation. Confirm lyric constraints have informed viable phrasing without silently prescribing a particular musical organization. Remove compliance language and narrative-scene explanations from the final Style paragraph.

## Decision gate before counting

For an open sound brief, check that the chosen organization reflects an actual exploration of suitable musical possibilities when the catalog is accessible. Describe materially different organizations through their time, pitch, parts or form; instrument substitution or a label alone is not the comparison. A specific brief needs no artificial alternatives. If a source is unavailable, do not claim to have consulted it.

For intended singing, verify that the proposed verse includes a concrete pitched melodic behavior selected for this song, rather than a generic claim that it is melodic. Verify that the text's line length, recurrence and scene have not been treated as a genre or arrangement instruction. Preserve a deliberate match when it fits the user's brief. Remove private selection or compliance language from the Style paragraph; keep the audible choices.

## Sound-content check

Privately check that the paragraph gives the chosen direction an audible identity: how it moves, what carries melody and harmony, how its selected parts interact, and how the voice is performed. The important details vary by song. Remove lyric-scene explanations and internal compliance commentary. A precise tempo or setting may be a chosen musical proposal; do not present a guessed number as a measured source fact. Retain musical choices the current brief actually requires.

Confirm an intended sung verse has a selected pitched melodic behavior. Confirm that a repeated lyric phrase was not automatically turned into the same arrangement decision used for other songs. Necessary avoidances go in EXCLUDE. Count the final Style paragraph after all edits and report the result actually measured.

## Final delivery

Finished lyrics unchanged. Intended singing includes recurring pitched verse or verse-equivalent behavior; actual refrains only. Speech, chant, whisper and shouts need explicit intent or intentional tags. Artist/work/geographic/persona shortcuts and researchIDs do not enter complete Style examples or final Style. The writer never requests or evaluates audio.

The response has exactly STYLE OF MUSIC, CHARACTER COUNT, EXCLUDE, RATIONALE SONG TITLE. Style is one positive self-contained paragraph of exactly 1,000 characters; exact final count is tool-verified after all edits. Necessary avoidances stay separate and may be none; title is brief and original. No citation, listening field or extra commentary. If verification is unavailable, disclose the unmet gate rather than inventing compliance.
