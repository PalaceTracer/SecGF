# Optional researched influence profiles

Resource-level scope: profile-specific choices and cautions belong to that proposed bundle, not universal laws. Read selectively for a concrete question; do not inherit its entire arrangement.

Use a profile only when a specific musical question makes it useful or the current brief requests the relationship. These anonymous bundles are original arrangement proposals, not verified reconstructions, confirmed isolated favorites, genre categories, candidate slots, a rotation or tested Suno commands. A profile may refine or reshape a direction; none is required. Catalog discovery remains available before choosing accompaniment.

Transfer a useful relationship only when justified by the current song. Do not inherit the entire source, tempo, melody or section sequence. Current intent and confirmed preferences take priority. Never include profile handles in Style. Detailed upstream production evidence is incomplete; these descriptions must not be presented as recording findings.

## Selectable relationship studies

| Handle and architecture | Defining audible relationships | Fits when the finished lyric or brief needs | Variation knobs and conflict boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **I01 — Nimble melody, short replies** | Definite short pitched verse cells carry dense words over clipped rhythm; a bright instrumental reply waits for line endings. The refrain preserves melodic identity while lengthening its destination vowel. | Quick-moving, playful or direct text has clear thought endings and an actual recurring phrase. | Choose stepwise motion or one local leap, straight or uneven small attacks, and reply source. Coordinate diction and reply timing; simultaneous independent activity is also possible. Do not turn buoyancy into speech or force cheerful harmony. |
| **I02 — Independent instrumental motor** | A repeating guitar, keyboard or other pitched figure supplies motion beneath a separately shaped sung tune. The figure may continue independently, double selected notes, shift register or rest according to phrase timing and intended attention. | Continuous momentum is wanted, but verse and refrain need distinct melodic identities. | Alter motor attack, subdivision, register or source while keeping its recognizable behavior. Co-equal hooks are eligible when their phrase relation is intentional; do not assume they must be reduced to protect singing. |
| **I03 — Piano continuity, programmed motion** | A small piano motif remains recognizable as economical programmed percussion enters; the lead retains a definite recurring pitched identity while force, duration and ensemble activity may remain stable or change. | The brief wants acoustic attacks and precise programmed motion; neither opening scale nor emotional arc is implied. | Choose exposed or supported piano entry, pulse density, low-end support and the location of projection. Do not require simultaneous growth in tempo, layer count, range and width; piano intimacy never implies spoken delivery. |
| **I04 — Foreground bass identity** | An articulated, optionally roughened bass figure carries recognition; drum attacks establish the frame and upper parts may support, interlock or share attention. Sung phrases retain foreground clarity through selective bass gaps or register separation. | The brief wants physical low/midrange character or a groove that is recognizable without many layers. | Decide unified kick/bass impact versus anchor-and-reply, short versus sustaining notes, and how much edge belongs to bass. Several active hooks need a purposeful register, timing or blend relationship; enormous sub tails are not a prerequisite. |
| **I05 — Percussion continuity, changed weight** | One distinctive attack pattern links contrasting textures; a real section changes accent spacing or small-note activity while a clear sung motif survives. Bass either reinforces those anchors or replies selectively. | The lyric has a genuine turn, and the brief wants continuity through a rhythmic contrast. | Change felt weight, subdivision or grouping only where phrasing supports it. Distinguish a fixed-tempo half-time impression from an actual tempo change. Do not add a new meter or every-beat kick merely to use this profile. |
| **I06 — Planned vocal hierarchy** | A pitched foreground lead carries the text; lower and upper voices can sustain, move independently or coordinate the same text. Support may be continuous or localized, with clear ownership of the recurring tune. | The user wants layered singing, sustained grandeur or complementary registers around a repeated declaration. | Choose unison reinforcement, harmony or independent response deliberately; vary entrances and reserve layer reduction for an exposed word. Use additional voices when requested or justified as an original choice in an open brief; it must not manufacture a duet narrative or crowd dense verses. |
| **I07 — Contained verse, earned melodic opening** | A clear recurring verse contour uses restrained force. The refrain broadens its pitch span or note length; one late held destination follows a deliberately placed accompaniment breath. | The brief calls for a contained-to-open melodic contrast and the actual structure supports it; guarded text alone does not select this arc. | Choose the intended opening through interval, register, duration, harmony or support width; one or several coordinated changes may serve it. Scope a pause to backing, lead or all layers. Do not demand a belt, key change, high note and full cut together, or reduce the verse to conversation. |
| **I08 — Instrumental continuation, shared foreground** | A source that supported the sung passage develops its hook during an existing wordless gap; piano, strings, guitar or electronic tone exchange or briefly share foreground without adding lyric. | A lyric thought benefits from musical continuation, and the text or brief allows an instrumental passage. | Choose handoff, answer, shared contour or coequal lines, plus what keeps the pulse. If the lyrics provide no gap, make any proposed interlude explicit in private planning; do not silently add a new section or invent words. |
| **I09 — Clear chord identity, local grouping contrast** | Distinct chord attacks keep harmonic changes legible; a quieter sustained layer supplies atmosphere. One real contrast passage can adopt a rolling grouping before returning to the established pulse. | The brief values harmonic movement or the supplied phrase lengths support differing rhythmic groupings. | Keep only the chord-clarity relation when grouping change is unnecessary. Choose sustain behind attacks, chord-change rate and one connecting motif. Do not smear every harmonic source or claim an exact meter without a written reason. |
| **I10 — Acoustic attacks, electronic sustain** | A plucked or struck acoustic part supplies tactile rhythm while an electronic bed sustains harmony. One motif changes carrier or is locally doubled at a return; the pitched tune retains its intended role in the selected recording image. | The user wants both source families to have meaningful roles, or a lyric contrasts immediacy with expansion. | Swap which family carries hook versus bed, alter envelope or register, and select the handoff event. Continuous doubling may deliberately create a composite timbre; independent roles may instead create complementarity. Specify the intended relationship rather than stacking style labels. |
| **I11 — Cut, changed weight, recognizable return** | A completed sung phrase leads to a short explicitly scoped interruption; the return restores a recognizable hook with a changed low-end envelope, accent weight or carrier. | The lyric has a decisive arrival and the brief calls for abrupt electronic or band contrast. | Choose a drum rest, accompaniment cut or true all-sound silence; choose one return change. Do not import a source example's vocal mode, assume a drop belongs in every song, or leave long tails while requesting total silence. |
| **I12 — Melodic identity through withdrawal** | The same definite sung contour remains as drums, bass or backing voices withdraw in stages. A continuing motif or held tone connects the exposed final phrase to earlier material. | Withdrawal is the intended ending behavior; reflective or unresolved meaning alone does not prescribe it. | Change decay, accompaniment register, remaining source and the order of exits. Preserve singing after the bed thins; do not add an obligatory final refrain or fade when the words need a clean endpoint. |

These are not twelve genre categories, recording reconstructions or complete Style templates. A profile's fit column is a musical reason to consider it, not a prediction of what the user must enjoy. Source choices, force, tempo and harmonic vocabulary remain adjustable. Existing guitar-and-drum, groove-centered, sparse ensemble, heavy, spacious and other guide mechanisms remain available beyond this bank.
