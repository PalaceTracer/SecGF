# 02 — Musical organization: vocabulary and song-specific judgment

## Foundation, not a profile menu

Musical organization coordinates time, pitch, parts, voices, form and recording image. Begin with the current brief and confirmed preferences, not a complete example arrangement. A provisional direction explains how the music will operate for this song. It is revisable after useful research. Neither familiar nor unfamiliar approaches receive automatic priority.

An **attribute** is a variable such as attack, register or duration. A **role** states what a part does. A **relationship** explains how parts or events interact. A **scope** states where it applies. A **proposal** is an intended implementation, not a source fact or heard result. Musical specificity comes from these relationships rather than a pile of labels.

For a candidate phrase ask: what object does it modify; on which dimension; relative to what; at which actual phrase or event; and is that information worth its character cost? “Short bass notes answer the kick between main arrivals” gives more than “driving bass.” A sustained low tone may be equally specific when continuity rather than reply is wanted. No short-note, small-chord-set or sparse-accompaniment preference is universal.

## Diagnostic dimensions

Use only consequential questions. This is not a checklist to fill, taxonomy, candidate roster or collection of mandatory Style clauses. Distinctions may combine, remain stable, transform or be irrelevant.

| Domain | Decisions that can differ materially | Relationship to make concrete |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared time | Recurring pulse, singer-led flexible time, pulse-free phrase pacing; stable or changing meter | Who supplies time, and who follows or crosses it? |
| Subdivision and accents | Even or unequal smaller attacks, sparse or active subdivisions, aligned or displaced cycles | An upper figure may cross bar lines while the low pulse stays regular; no tempo change is implied. |
| Percussion and bass | Optional percussion; bass anchor, motor, reply, pedal, walking motion or independent melody | Separate onset, pitch destination and sustain. Harmony can be defined by voice and bass without a continuous chord pad. |
| Harmony | Cyclic, pedal-based, cadence-led or traveling; harmonic rhythm and voicing distinct | Upper tones may move slowly while lower voices change, or a fixed instrumental rhythm may carry changing harmony. |
| Melody and phrase | Stepwise or leaping contour, repeated cell, extension, contraction, unequal phrase length, motif transformation | Phrase endings need not coincide with bars or production blocks. For intended singing retain recurring pitched verse behavior. |
| Melodic relationship | Unison, octave reinforcement, chordal texture, answer, independent simultaneous line or imitation | Distinguish a gap reply from counterpoint; deliberate overlap can carry the argument. |
| Ensemble | One foreground, shared tune, interdependent equals, inner-line melody, rotating ownership or section exchanges | A single instrument can do several jobs; several sources can form one part. Neither source count nor layer count defines organization. |
| Vocals | Solo, duet, collective melody, alternating existing phrases; register, force, duration, articulation and harmony independent | Tune may be internal to a voiced ensemble. Collective energy does not imply chant or shout. Protect actual words and mode. |
| Form | Strophic, actual refrain-based, through-developing, episodic, return-with-variation, cadence-led or another suitable organization | Distinguish existing textual recurrence from a proposed instrumental relation. Never manufacture repeated words. |
| Development | Stasis, inflection, accumulation, subtraction, continuous transformation, interruption or contrast | Repeated words may stay musically unchanged; changing words may use the same stanza tune. Growth is not obligatory. |
| Recording image | Near/far, narrow/wide, direct/reflected, sharp/soft attack, short/long persistence, clean/rough | Allocate the image to roles and moments; intentional blend and articulated separation are both available. |

The familiar strategy of one persistent identity plus one meaningful change remains useful for some songs. It is not the planning default. Coherence can instead come from interdependent lines, stable ritual repetition, a sequence of evolving cadences, changing episodes, continuous timbral process or another justified organization. These are explanatory possibilities, not replacement profile slots. Do not compare all of them on every song.

## How dimensions interact

- **Pulse is not activity.** A slower sung arc can cross fast broken percussion; a lively melody can work without a repeating drum beat. Specify the layer carrying motion.
- **Rhythmic placement is not harmonic destination.** Bass can retain its onset pattern while selected chord-boundary notes change. Root position is not automatically tonic closure.
- **Hook is not always a top-line refrain.** Recognition can lie in an inner vocal part, bass figure, rhythmic gesture, recurring interval relation or a transforming cell. No catchy-hook obligation follows for every brief.
- **Counterpoint is not a fill.** Independent lines may sound together. Contrast register, articulation, duration or phrase peaks when word priority matters; do not automatically silence every other melody.
- **Density is not source count.** Overlapping sustain may fill more than many short coordinated events. Complex ensemble writing need not be clutter; a small ensemble need not be simple.
- **Vocal force is not pitch.** Soft, close, syllabic and crisp remain definitely sung when the contour is stated. Higher notes, more grain, more voices and longer vowels are separate choices.
- **Narrative does not prescribe style.** Sadness need not select slow minor harmony; confidence need not select a belt or large chorus; intimacy need not select acoustic sparsity. Choose a supported interpretation, including deliberate tension between words and music when intended.
- **Form can run on several timescales.** A sequenced cell may continue through lyric headings; harmony may arrive after an irregular sung phrase. Do not impose a change at every boundary.

## Compatibility without automatic simplification

| Apparent conflict | Coherent design possibility | Check in the written plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flexible singer and precise rhythm | Voice phrases across a stable backing | Distinguish this from the whole ensemble following the singer. |
| Two independent melodies and clear words | Different registers or durations, coordinated phrase peaks | Each line has a job; no assumed universal gap-only rule. |
| Many voices and a discernible tune | Internal tune with supporting outer parts or rotating foreground | Identify melody ownership and actual text allocation. |
| Held upper note and moving harmony | Compatible common tone, or deliberate controlled tension | Release or adapt it if the intended chord relation no longer fits. |
| Root-led bass and open ending | Clear foundation under an unresolved sung/harmonic endpoint | A chord's root is not necessarily the tonal home. |
| Wide backing and close lead | Width belongs behind a stable direct vocal | Global “wide, dry, distant, near” lacks allocation. |
| Stable form and expressive development | Words or articulation carry meaning inside a fixed frame | Do not invent a lift to prove development. |
| Dense arrangement and restrained voice | Ensemble keeps useful register/attack allocation around pitched words | Do not equate softness with sparse or spoken. |

True contradictions concern the same object, time and dimension: unaccompanied solo versus continuous chordal ensemble, total silence versus a continuing tail, fixed harmony versus a new chord every beat. Resolve by current priorities or explicit scope; do not average into vague adjectives.

## Musical direction and genre discovery

Choose an actual musical direction rather than an unspecified neutral accompaniment. An open brief permits suitable electronic, amplified-band, acoustic, ensemble-led or other organizations; these are illustrative possibilities, not slots or quotas. Genre/style names can be useful descriptors when their defining relationships are supplied. No rule requires the user to name a genre before it can be considered. Artist/work names and geographic shorthand remain inappropriate substitutes for sound.

Do not choose acoustic guitar, a slow few-chord loop, a narrow verse, root-only bass or dry sparse production merely because the words are narrative. The same relevance standard applies to every familiar design. Dense singing can inhabit continuous electronic or band activity; articulate arrangement need not be thin. Retain any of these choices when they actually serve this brief.

## Selection and optional enrichment

Follow the single workflow in [10](10-style-compiler.md). A tight brief can proceed directly. If meaningful choices remain, consider organizations that differ in time, pitch, part responsibility or form, not just instrument names. No fixed candidate count. Compare requirements, lyric/performance fit, musical coherence, useful distinctiveness and character cost. A familiar design can win.

Use [catalog discovery](../optional/catalog/EXPANDED_SONG_STYLE_CATALOG.md) while forming a direction when it can widen useful consideration; a previously chosen arrangement is not a prerequisite. Retrieve complete [profiles](../optional/INFLUENCE_PROFILES.md) or [specialist notes](../optional/SPECIALIST_NOTES.md) for relevant craft questions. An original proposal and a reference-derived relationship face the same fit test. Research can reshape the provisional direction; do not keep a token influence solely to demonstrate consultation. If selected, express its musical behavior in Style. A useful established genre/style descriptor may accompany those relationships; labels alone are insufficient, and internal researchIDs remain excluded. Missing catalog membership is not lack of permission.

Privately distinguish present instructions, confirmed taste, documented practice, tentative inference and invention. Use [11](11-written-design-audit.md) to question unexplained inheritance while retaining fitting similarities. Every intended sung verse keeps definite recurring pitched behavior; special modes need current intent or an intentional tag. No audio or generated-file evaluation enters this route.
